What “immersive learning” means here

Children should act inside a believable illustrated world: sort objects, build, measure, trade, investigate or influence a story. The learning action should change something visible. Avoid simply placing a multiple-choice worksheet over attractive scenery.

Strong visuals should clarify the action, not overwhelm the task. A coherent world, friendly characters, clear feedback and readable controls matter more than the number of backgrounds or menu screens.

Ideas under discussion

  • Living Village: help shops, gardens, bridges and water systems through practical learning missions.
  • Lost Island Expedition: explore, repair routes and discover new habitats.
  • Little Inventors Workshop: build and test small machines and structures.
  • Story Explorers: act inside illustrated stories using reading, listening and evidence.
  • PAWS & PATHS: an original kitten helps friends by changing the world; the latest cat-focused discussion idea.

Discussion recommendation, not a selection: a Living Village with some island-like exploration could begin with one shop mission at several difficulty levels. The owner has not chosen a concept, platform, theme or final feature list.

Earlier exploratory work

The LearningGarden source starter delivered on 24 October contains Count Apples, Find Shapes and Memory Pairs, original art/audio, gentle feedback and local anonymous visit counters. It was based on a narrower provisional age assumption; the new Classes 1–5 goal supersedes that audience assumption.

Its 72 package/source checks passed, but Unity compilation, Play mode, native C# checks and device builds remain unverified. English-only; no narration, portrait production UX, accounts or leaderboard. Reuse useful systems only after the concept is chosen and the code is verified.
