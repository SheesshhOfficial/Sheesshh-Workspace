Game & Architecture

Current implemented behavior, editable presentation and remaining product work—not the old planning-only proposal.

1. Audience and city loop

Current scope: Class 1 English, portrait-oriented illustrated 2D, five floating-island houses. Classes 2–5 and additional cities are future content work, not completed curricula.

  1. Start at Home and open the Cloud City route.
  2. Select the next available house; the cat travels in the tiny airship.
  3. Meet the customer and begin that house’s activity.
  4. Answer three questions; use a hint and retry when needed.
  5. Deliver the letter, celebrate and continue to the next house.
  6. Revisit completed houses for fresh practice without duplicate completion rewards.

2. Five implemented activities

  1. Number Line: values 1–20; before, after and missing-number questions.
  2. Count Birds: quantities 1–10. Birds enter and settle before answers are enabled.
  3. Shape Match: circle, square, triangle and rectangle; readable geometry rather than colour-only clues.
  4. Picture–Word Match: cat, dog, sun, fish and bus, with English word choices.
  5. Add/Subtract: quantities and results in the 0–10 range, with visual counters.

Each house contains three questions and each question has three unique, shuffled choices. The editable visual pools include seven Number Line labels, ten bird slots with count-driven visibility, four shape variants, and ten-circle counter pools: addition uses two groups and subtraction crosses out removed counters. Content correctness checks do not replace educator review of vocabulary, difficulty and curriculum fit.

3. Rules and local progress

GameSession manages phases, route unlocks, answer handling, delivery and replay. QuestionGenerator creates the activity content and choices. GameController connects the rules to local saved progress and settings. Transition tokens and answer guards protect repeated callbacks; replay preserves completed-house status without granting another first-delivery reward.

Hints and retries are gentle; hearts celebrate delivery, not health or lives. Local progress is implemented; cross-device accounts or cloud sync are not part of this slice.

4. Editable presentation—not rebuilt layouts

The scene Assets/COD/Editable/Authoring/CODEditableGame.unity contains persistent prefab instances. CODEditablePresenter binds existing named objects to session content, buttons and visibility rather than reconstructing the screen layout on each transition.

The twelve screen prefabs are Home, Map, Greeting, NumberLine, CountBirds, ShapeMatch, PictureWordMatch, AddSubtract, HintModal, Delivery, Complete and Settings. A separate Shell provides shared controls. A theme asset supplies authoring defaults; existing Text, Image and RectTransform components remain directly editable.

Animation uses nested Visual children so designer-owned anchors retain their placement. The create-once Editor builder refuses to overwrite the authored output directory. The legacy CODGame.unity and runtime-generated UI remain a rollback checkpoint, not the preferred editing workflow.

5. Art and audio boundary

Original generated art is integrated alongside procedural elements. Five independently configured houses currently reuse the cottage painting; the bear remains a placeholder. Pip now has six transparent greeting frames at 6 fps in the latest local version. Other recipients are not covered by that new animation.

English text is present. A 379-clip narration batch has been prepared but is not integrated or audibly quality-checked. Do not demonstrate an unavailable audio control as a finished feature.

6. Acceptance still required

  • Verify persistent layout changes outside Play and avoid overwriting owner edits.
  • Run real mouse/touch interaction, interruption/resume and device performance checks.
  • Integrate narration with matching text, replay, mute and reduced-motion behavior.
  • Validate actual Windows and Android release artifacts separately; Editor success is not a shipping-build claim.
